# Reviewer Guide

Path: `backend/tests/evaluation/review/reviewer_guide.md`
Guide version 0.2.0, for review packets v0.2. Date: 2026-11-14.

## In short

- REIA is software that helps real estate agents remember and understand their customer conversations. We test it on **invented** chats and call notes.
- We ask you to check whether these texts sound **natural and realistic** in your language and your market.
- Everything is fictional. There are no real people, clients or transactions.
- It takes roughly 45 to 60 minutes in total (this is an estimate). You can do it in parts, and you can review fewer scenarios than we send.
- Taking part is **voluntary and unpaid**. You can stop at any time without giving a reason.

Thank you for considering this. Your independent judgment is the part of this process that software cannot replace.

## 1. About the project

REIA (Real Estate Relationship Intelligence Agent) is a tool for real estate agents. It reads the conversations an agent has with customers, remembers what each customer wants, and helps the agent prepare for calls and write follow-up messages. The agent always stays in control. REIA does not contact customers by itself.

To test REIA we need realistic conversations. We cannot use real client conversations, so we use **synthetic** ones: invented chats between a fictional customer and a fictional agent. They were drafted with the help of AI tools, and AI tools were also used for a first round of checks. AI checks are **not** a substitute for a native or fluent professional. That is why we are asking you.

## 2. What we ask you to do

You will receive three things:

1. This guide.
2. A **packet** for your language, with several short scenarios.
3. A **feedback form**.

The work has three parts.

| Part | What you do | When |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1: Read and answer | Read each scenario and answer a few questions about what you understood: what the customer wants, what the agent promised, what is unclear | First. **Before** you open the intended meanings |
| Part 2: Review | Open the intended meanings (a separate section at the end of the packet, after a page break). Then rate and review the language and realism, and list any issues | After Part 1 is finished for all scenarios |
| Part 3: General questions | Answer a few general questions about your market | Last |

Why this order: we want to know if a normal reader understands the text the way we intended. If you saw the intended meaning first, we could not tell. Please do not open the intended meanings until you have written your Part 1 answers for all scenarios.

In some places the packet shows two versions of a message (Version 1 and Version 2). These are **choice items**. Pick the one that sounds more natural, or write your own version.

Step by step:

1. Read one scenario.
2. Write your Part 1 answers for it in the form.
3. Repeat for every scenario you want to review.
4. Open the intended meanings.
5. For each scenario, give your ratings and list issues.
6. Answer the general questions.
7. Send the form back (section 8).

## 3. Which language we need

| Packet | What we need from you |
|---|---|
| Spanish (Spain), `es-ES` | Spanish as written by people in Spain, in the way agents and customers use WhatsApp, calls and email |
| Portuguese (Portugal), `pt-PT` | European Portuguese as used in Portugal |
| English | English as used by real estate professionals and international clients in the UAE (many customers are not native English speakers) |

Please do not judge the text against other variants. If something sounds like another variant, for example Latin American Spanish or Brazilian Portuguese, **flag it**. We want to know about it.

## 4. What to look for

| Area | Questions to keep in mind |
|---|---|
| Natural phrasing | Would a person really write this? Does it sound like a translation from English? |
| Regional language | Is the vocabulary, grammar and spelling right for your country? |
| Real estate terms | Are the terms the ones agents and customers really use? Is any term used wrongly? |
| Register | Is the level of formality right (for example how people address each other)? Is the text too polished compared with real chat messages? |
| Customer intent | Are the wishes, doubts and objections believable? |
| Agent commitments | Are the promises and next steps the kind an agent really makes? |
| Consistency | Do numbers, dates, days of the week, names and who-says-what agree across the messages? |
| Real data | Does anything look like a real person, company, address or phone number? |

## 5. How to mark issues

### 5.1 Message references

Each scenario has an ID and a revision, for example `ES-01 r2`. Conversations are lettered (A, B, C) and messages are numbered. A reference looks like `ES-01 r2 A3`: scenario, revision, conversation A, message 3. Please always use this format, so we can find the exact place.

### 5.2 Severity

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blocker | The text is wrong for your variant, misleading or impossible to understand |
| Major | Clearly unnatural, a wrong term, or inconsistent facts |
| Minor | A matter of style or taste |

### 5.3 Required or preference

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Required | The text needs this change to be correct or natural |
| Preference | You would change it, but the text is acceptable without the change |

### 5.4 Suggest the smallest change

Please suggest a minimal correction, for example one word or one phrase. You do not need to rewrite whole messages. If a situation is unrealistic, just tell us why.

## 6. What we are not asking

- We are **not** asking you to judge how well REIA works. That is tested separately.
- We are **not** asking for legal, tax or financial advice. You only tell us whether terms such as a contract, a tax or a fee are used the way people really use them in language.
- We are **not** asking you to rewrite the scenarios.
- Please **do not use AI tools** to write or check your answers. We already use AI checks. What we need from you is your own independent judgment. If you used any tool at all, even a dictionary or a translator, please say so in the form.
- Please **do not include real client information** in your feedback. Speak in general terms about your market.

## 7. Confidentiality and your data

- All content is fictional.
- Please do not publish or share the packets. They are test data, and sharing them could weaken the tests.
- Your name and contact details are kept privately by the project owner and are **not** stored with the review data. You are referred to only by a code, for example `R-ES-01`.
- Together with your feedback we record only: your code, your language and variant, whether you are a native or fluent speaker, whether you work in real estate, the region you know best, and the time you spent.
- We will **not** name you or say that you took part without your written permission. If you agree to be named, we will confirm the exact wording with you first.
- If you accidentally include real client information, tell us. We will remove it and will not store it.
- Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time. If you withdraw, we will not use your feedback.
- There is no payment, no contract and no obligation.

## 8. Returning your feedback

- Complete the feedback form and send it back **to the person who sent you this guide**, by the same way you received it. A document, or the text of an email, is fine.
- You may write in Spanish, Portuguese or English.
- There is no deadline. If you can return it within about two weeks, that helps us plan, but there is no pressure.
- If you cannot finish everything, send what you have. Partial feedback is useful. Please say which scenarios you reviewed.
- If you have questions, reply to the person who sent you this guide.

## 9. A short example

This example is **not** one of your scenarios. It only shows how to fill in the form.

**Message:**

> EX-01 r1 A1 [10:02] Anna (customer): Hi Tom, our budget is around 300 thousand, but we could stretch a bit for the right flat. Two bedrooms, close to a station.

**Part 1 answer (written before seeing any intended meaning):**

> The customer wants a 2-bedroom flat close to a station. The budget is about 300,000 and she says it could go higher for the right flat. The currency is not mentioned. The agent has not promised anything yet.

**Part 2 issue row:**

| Reference | Severity | Required or preference | Type of issue | What you would write instead | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-01 r1 A1 | Minor | Preference | Unclear information | "around 300,000 euros" | The currency is missing. In real chats people often leave it out, so this may be fine. |

## 10. Questions you may have

**I am a native speaker but I do not work in real estate. Can I still help?**
Yes. Language naturalness is very valuable. Tell us your background in the form, and skip questions about terminology if you are unsure.

**Do I need to review all the scenarios?**
No. Review as many as you comfortably can and tell us which ones.

**May I answer in my own language?**
Yes. Spanish, Portuguese or English are all fine.

**What if two people would say the same thing differently in my country?**
Tell us. Say what you would use and, if you know, which region or group uses which version.

**What if something sounds like Latin American Spanish or Brazilian Portuguese?**
Flag it as a regional variant issue and tell us what you would write instead.

**What if I think a scenario is not realistic?**
Tell us why and what is different in real life. That is useful information.

**What if the text looks like it is about a real person?**
All names and details are invented. Any resemblance is a coincidence. Please tell us if something looks real, so we can change it.

**What if I disagree with the "intended meaning"?**
Tell us. That is exactly the kind of feedback we need.

## 11. Terms used in the packet and form

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Synthetic | Invented for testing. No real person or transaction |
| Scenario | A short set of invented messages or notes about one fictional customer |
| Blind comprehension | Your own description of what the text says, written before you see the intended meaning |
| Intended meaning | What the text was written to express. Shown after Part 1 |
| Choice item | A message shown in two versions for you to choose from |
| Blocker, Major, Minor | Severity of an issue (section 5.2) |
| Required, Preference | How necessary a change is (section 5.3) |
| Regional variant flag | A note that wording belongs to another variant, for example Latin American Spanish or Brazilian Portuguese |
